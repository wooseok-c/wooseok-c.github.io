About

I'm an undergraduate at Konkuk University (B.S. Industrial Engineering, expected 2027.02) and a research intern in Prof. Jeong-Woo Sohn's lab at Sungkyunkwan University.

Three questions so far: what one cortical area sends to another during reaching and when, how much decoding performance survives when a large transformer decoder is compressed into a spiking network, and whether the regional structure these models appear to learn is real.

What I want to build is a neuro foundation model that holds across modalities. Spikes, fMRI and EEG each come with their own recording unit, so a model built around one of them does not carry to the others.

I also re-implement architectures from their papers. That is in section 04.

Research

Simultaneous primate M1 / DLPFC spike recordings, decoded with POYO.

What DLPFC sends to M1, and when

in progress
  • Simultaneous DLPFC, M1 and PPC recordings from two macaques reaching to targets that sometimes jump mid-trial
  • Extract the DLPFC–M1 communication subspace with reduced-rank regression, then test what stays decodable from it once kinematics are controlled for
  • Ask whether DLPFC's influence on M1 is fixed or depends on M1's current state, and how long error information takes to reach DLPFC
  • Shuffle nulls, control regions, held-out sessions, and synthetic data with a known answer to check the methods can detect what they claim to. The subspace decomposition is correlational, so it does not support a causal claim, and the analysis is written to keep that boundary explicit

Compressing a neural foundation model into a spiking decoder

first author · BioCAS 2026 (accepted)
  • Compressed an 11.8 M-parameter transformer decoder into a 49.8 K-parameter spiking neural network (LSNN), a 237× reduction
  • Characterized the limits of representation transfer using CKA, FitNet-style hint training, and knowledge distillation
  • Measured how well on-chip adaptation with e-prop tracks drift, and where it fails
11.8M → 49.8K parameters · 237× reduction

Regional representation in multi-region decoders

first author · SfN 2026
  • Ablated regions from a multi-region transformer decoder and traced what changed at the readout
  • In this decoder, decodability and attention weights turned out to be weak evidence for regional representation: both stayed high in conditions where the region itself contributed little
  • The analysis is correlational and does not settle the mechanism. What it supports is caution when regional structure is read off a decoder, not a claim about where encoding happens

Learning by re-implementation

Published architectures that I re-implemented in PyTorch from the papers, module by module, without a reference implementation open, as a way of studying them. None of these are my own designs. Each one has a test that checks shapes end to end.

Roughly in the order I worked through them.

Swin is the only full model here rather than a single block. It comes to 28.3M parameters; the paper's Table 1 reports 29M. I kept tensors in grid form (B, H, W, C) instead of the official flattened layout, which makes the window shift easier to follow.
